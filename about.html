About

Security research with the mechanism left in.

I am an independent security researcher. This site is my research log: Windows internals, reverse engineering, network telemetry, and detection engineering.

I reverse engineer how software moves data across trust boundaries, then write down the parts that help defenders: object layouts, protocol state machines, trust-store changes, and the artifacts that survive cleanup. The work ranges from service and kernel interfaces to browser and network protocols, malware tradecraft, and endpoint telemetry. The goal is to make a system legible, not to turn it into a turnkey intrusion tool.

Scope and disclosure

Research is tested on systems I own or have written permission to use. Vulnerabilities are reported privately before publication. I do not publish credential theft code, exploit chains, persistence recipes, or ready-to-run intrusion tooling.

Some posts discuss dual-use Windows primitives. When a technique could be reused by an infostealer or another intruder, the post focuses on the mechanism and the defensive signal, and deliberately omits the operational sequence that would make it a recipe.

This site

I publish under the handle Portbuster. The site is static HTML and CSS. It uses system fonts only, has no analytics or trackers, and loads no third-party assets. The source is published at github.com/portbuster1337/portbuster1337.github.io.

Contact

Corrections, detection improvements, and build reports are welcome through GitHub. If you are reporting a vulnerability in a third-party product, contact the vendor first.
